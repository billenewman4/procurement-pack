We're building a desk air-quality monitor — and procuring every part through our own pack

The dogfood project from the whiteboard session, now real: one Arduino, a CO₂ sensor, a particulate sensor, a little screen — every part found, ordered, and tracked through the procurement pack, so we feel exactly what Clark and Harry will feel.

The build

A small open-frame gadget that sits on a desk and shows live air quality: an Arduino reads a real NDIR CO₂ sensor and a laser PM2.5 particulate sensor and paints the numbers on a 0.96" OLED. One USB-C cable powers everything. No soldering where we can avoid it (I²C/Qwiic chaining), no 3D-printed parts, ~$100-ish budget, US vendors, fast shipping.

Remember why we picked something this small: the point isn't the gadget, it's generating real procurement — real multi-vendor orders, real confirmation emails hitting Gmail, and ideally at least one part that fails so the diagnose flow earns its keep. Build it, break it, fix it — all through the pack.
